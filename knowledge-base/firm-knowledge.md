### 5.2 Property & Real Estate Law
**Tagline:** Title verification, KDA/SBCA & land disputes

Property fraud is Karachi's most expensive legal risk. Every engagement starts with forensic verification of title — Sub-Registrar certified copies, revenue records (fard / intiqal), SBCA plan approvals and encumbrance searches across courts and banks. When disputes occur, the firm litigates possession claims, specific performance of agreements to sell, inheritance partitions and cancellation of fraudulent registries before the High Court of Sindh and City Courts Karachi.

**Services offered:**
- **Property Title Verification** — A 13-point due-diligence report covering chain of title, mutations, taxes, mortgages and litigation status, delivered in 3–5 working days.
- **Registry & Transfer Execution** — Drafting and registration of sale deeds, gift deeds and Wakala Namas before Karachi Sub-Registrars.
- **SBCA & Builder Matters** — Plan approvals, regularization, builder-defect claims and handover/possession disputes for apartments.
- **Land Dispute Litigation** — Possession suits, specific performance, partition of inherited land and cancellation of forged documents.
- **Housing Society Issues** — Bahria Town, DHA, KDA and cooperative society allotments, transfers and membership disputes.
- **Landlord & Tenant** — Rent agreements and eviction petitions before Rent Controllers under the Sindh Rented Premises Ordinance.

**Courts/Forums:** High Court of Sindh, City Courts Karachi, Malir Courts, Sub-Registrar Offices, SBCA (Sindh Building Control Authority).

**Key Urdu/legal terms:**
- *Fard-e-Malkiat* — Official revenue record showing ownership of land.
- *Intiqal* — Mutation; revenue entry recording transfer of title.
- *Bayana* — Earnest money paid on an agreement to purchase property.

**FAQs:**
- **What documents do I need for property transfer in Karachi?** Typically: original registered deed/allotment-cum-transfer letter, latest tax challans, seller's CNIC, approved building plan (if constructed), NOCs where applicable, and a lawyer-drafted sale deed executed before the Sub-Registrar with two witnesses.
- **How long does title verification take?** The standard written verification report takes 3–5 working days; complex agricultural or disputed parcels may take up to two weeks including court searches.
- **Is an unregistered agreement to sell valid?** It can evidence intent but does not convey title. Only a registered conveyance before the Sub-Registrar transfers ownership; courts can, however, enforce specific performance of written agreements.
- **Can I buy Bahria Town file-based property safely?** File-based purchases carry additional risk (allotment vs. actual plot). The firm verifies allotment letters, transfer records with the society, and any injunctions before advising on price and payment milestones.

### 6.1 Step-by-Step Guide to Property Verification in Karachi
*By Ahmed Rizvi, Advocate High Court — ~6 min read*

Buying property in Karachi without verification is the single most common cause of land litigation in Sindh. Before paying any token or bayana, complete the following checks:

1. **Collect the title documents** — registered sale deed/registry (or allotment + transfer letter for leased KDA plots), latest property tax challans, approved building plan (SBCA) for constructed units, seller's CNIC, and Wakala Nama if a power of attorney is involved.
2. **Verify at the Sub-Registrar office** — apply for a certified copy of the registered deed at the concerned Sub-Registrar (e.g., Clifton, Saddar, Korangi) to confirm the deed exists in the official register.
3. **Check mutation & revenue records** — for open land, inspect the Record of Rights (fard-e-malkiat) at the Board of Revenue/Mukhtiarkar office to confirm the seller appears as owner after the last mutation (intiqal).
4. **Confirm SBCA/builder status** — for apartments, verify the project is approved by the Sindh Building Control Authority and that the developer has no pending regularization issues.
5. **Search for encumbrances** — check for bank mortgages, court attachments (zabti) or injunction orders. The firm runs formal searches in the High Court of Sindh and City Courts Karachi as part of its due-diligence retainer.
6. **Execute a properly drafted transfer** — use a lawyer-drafted agreement to sell, then register the final conveyance; never rely on unregistered agreements alone.

*This guide is general information under Sindh law, not legal advice for a specific transaction.*

### 6.2 Understanding the Sindh Rented Premises Ordinance: Rights of Landlords & Tenants
*By Fatima Rizvi, Associate — ~5 min read*

The Sindh Rented Premises Ordinance, 1979 governs most residential and commercial tenancies in Karachi, enforced through the office of the Rent Controller.

- **Written tenancy agreement is mandatory** — every tenancy must be recorded in writing and submitted to the Rent Controller within seven days; an unregistered agreement weakens both parties' positions.
- **Landlord rights** — fair market rent revisions as permitted by the Ordinance; eviction on statutory grounds (non-payment, subletting without consent, structural damage, personal requirement); recovery of arrears through a rent eviction petition rather than self-help measures.
- **Tenant rights** — protection from eviction except on statutory grounds; receipts for every rent payment; reasonable notice before landlord inspection.
- **Eviction process** — a petition is filed before the Rent Controller (Karachi East/West/South/Central/Malir) with territorial jurisdiction; after service and evidence, the Controller decides within prescribed timelines; appeals lie to the Sindh High Court on questions of law.
- **Practical tips for landlords** — never collect more than one month's advance plus refundable security unless agreed in writing; keep CNIC copies of every occupant; issue monthly receipts, since courts treat undocumented cash rents harshly.

The firm regularly represents landlords and tenants before Rent Controllers across Karachi City Courts.

### 6.3 How to Obtain a Succession Certificate in Sindh
*By Ahmed Rizvi, Advocate High Court — ~4 min read*

When a person dies leaving movable assets (bank accounts, shares, prize bonds, etc.), legal heirs need a succession certificate issued by the civil/family judge with jurisdiction over where the deceased ordinarily resided.

1. **Gather core documents** — death certificate from NADRA/union council, CNICs of all legal heirs, NADRA Family Registration Certificate (FRC), and asset details (account numbers, share folios, bond serials).
2. **File the petition** — a petition under the Succession Act, 1925 is filed in the District Courts at Karachi (City Courts) or Malir, depending on residence; public notice is issued in newspapers inviting objections.
3. **Contested vs. uncontested** — most certificates are granted uncontested within roughly 6–10 weeks once the notice period expires; if a relative objects, the matter proceeds as a suit with evidence.
4. **Bond and issuance** — heirs furnish a surety bond equal to the asset value, after which certified copies are provided to each bank/institution.

*Note: immovable property does not pass via succession certificate — heirs use inheritance mutations (intiqal) and, where needed, declaration suits instead. The firm handles the entire process on a transparent, milestone-based fee schedule in PKR.*
